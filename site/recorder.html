Frame-time recorder

Every frame's time, split into CPU work and waiting, plus what the renderer did in that frame, written to a file on the console's drive and turned into an HTML report on your PC.

Turn it on

Use the Console settings tab (the Measure preset), or put one line in a file in valve/userconfig.d/ on the console:

host_frametime_analysis 2

0 is off (the default), 1 records only, 2 records and draws a frame-time graph and the work counters over the HUD. The setting is not saved to config.cfg on purpose: delete the line and the next boot records nothing.

Give the session a label that says what you are measuring:

host_frametime_label "office-run"

It goes into the header and the file name, cleaned to letters, digits, - and _ and cut to 22 characters.

What it records

  • Each frame goes into a ring in memory and is written to the drive every 5 seconds and at each map start. A power-off loses at most the last 5 seconds.
  • A session is written as segments: a new file at each map start, and when one reaches 8 MiB (about 3.3 minutes at 60 fps).
  • The header names the build; its "xbe" field is the XBE's link timestamp in hexadecimal, so a recording can always be matched to the build that made it.

Read the recordings

tools/frametime-report.py fetches the recordings from the console over FTP, prints a summary or lists sessions, and writes the HTML report. Load one session at a time: a whole folder of long recordings can exhaust memory.

Reading the numbers

An average frame rate hides the frames that hurt. Look at how many frames take longer than 33 ms (a dropped frame at 30 fps), the worst frames, and any stall over a quarter of a second. A session of half an hour at roughly 58 fps average with well under 2% of frames over 33 ms on most maps is a smooth one.

What the recordings showed and what was changed because of them is on the progress page. The full reference is docs/r3d/frametime-recorder.md in the repository.
