Black Mesa on the Xbox, step by step

Every optimisation step was played on the same console and recorded frame by frame. Three kinds of run: the route from Black Mesa Inbound into Unforeseen Consequences as a new game, and runs that start on c1a0c or c1a1b with the suit and every weapon. All numbers on this page are from the console, not the emulator.

CPU-bound throughout

The GPU wait stays near 0.4-0.5 ms on almost every map. Each gain came from taking work off the Pentium III: the C library's slow memcpy, model skinning and the water warp moved to the NV2A's vertex unit, cheaper network deltas, an exact integer sound mixer, a world pass that only visits visible faces, moving brushes that skip pointless collision traces, and a save that writes each file in one call.

The route now sits at the cap

From the first -O2 build to the #193 route, the average went from 42 to 58.9 fps. Every map now averages 56-60 fps, and the light maps idle at 5-6 ms of CPU: the saved CPU there is headroom, as the frame waits for the 60 Hz cap. c1a1b went from 42 to 57 fps once its water moved to the GPU and its moving brushes stopped tracing every object.

No stalls left over 250 ms

The #193 route (the deferred .sav write, cheaper player movement and entity decoding, the menu-font fixes) is the first run with no in-play frame over 250 ms. Autosave frames fell to 88-122 ms: the .sav is written over the next frames (at most ~19 ms each) and the thumbnail readback halved. The pause menu opens in 16-18 ms on every map. What is left: level loads of 1.1-2.2 s, where reading the first svc_clientdata and svc_resourcelist takes 0.5-0.9 s.

Before that

Autosaves dropped from 324-426 ms to 206-289 ms; on the console most of what remains is writing the .sav (123-136 ms) and the thumbnail readback (38 ms). Four other single-frame stalls of about 300 ms (in screen drawing and in reading a server message) showed up on c1a1b and c1a1c once the autosaves stopped hiding them; they are the next thing to look at.

Sound on the audio chip

The hardware-audio run (2026-10-21) plays the room effect, every sound, the speech and the music on the APU: 7,454 sounds on its voices, at most 47 of 64 at once, none sent back to the CPU, and shots started within 2 ms of their frame. Mixing on the CPU fell from 0.10-0.16 to 0.02-0.06 ms a frame and the music from 0.09 to 0.03 ms; driving the chip costs about 0.43 ms a frame, most of it a check that re-read the room kernel from uncached memory every update. With that check reading a copy in cached memory instead (#215, 2026-10-21), the chip's work per update fell from 225 to 98 µs and the tram maps c0a0 to c0a0d run 0.2-0.3 ms a frame lighter at the same fps; that run stops at c0a0e. Its 30 minutes run past the route to c1a1c with no stall over 250 ms; the 87-106 ms frames left are game saves.
